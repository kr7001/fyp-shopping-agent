1. The three research questions

Everything on this page exists to answer one of these.

RQ1 · PARTLY ANSWERED

How often does interaction-gated information occur, and in what forms?

Gated means the data is on the page but only appears after you interact with it. A listing shows one price; the price for the size you want appears only after clicking that size.

Two forms catalogued and built: price by variant and stock by variant. Frequency in the test shop turned out to be zero, but shops in real life like Shopee is not the case, so had to modify the test WebArena Environment.

RQ2 · ANSWERED

When a task needs gated information, does the agent retrieve it, and does it signal when it has not?

Two separate questions. Does it get the answer right, and when it does not, does it warn you.

Retrieves correctly 88% of the time on the 4-listing fixture and 58% at 15 listings, each quoted with its own denominator and never pooled. Signals uncertainty 0 times in 107 runs. The second half is the result that matters, because a caller cannot tell a guess from a checked answer.

RQ3 · FIRST HALF DONE

Can a question-conditioned retrieval policy reach correct answers with fewer interactions than exhaustive exploration?

Two halves. Knowing when you have looked enough, and knowing where to look so that enough costs less.

Both halves now built. The agent detects that its own answer is unsupported and refuses, and it chooses a retrieval strategy from the question before opening anything.

Together they match exhaustive retrieval on accuracy for 35 per cent fewer interactions and 33 per cent less money, with no wrong answers in 12 runs. That is the claim the question was written to make. It is in sample, because the policy was fixed after watching it fail, so a held-out run on a fresh catalogue is the next thing to spend money on.

The honest test

Two of the five rules were written after seeing failures they were meant to handle, so measuring them on those same failures proves nothing. The rules were locked at a recorded version, 7771e24, then fifteen fresh runs were made, then the rules were applied to those alone. A second held-out batch of twelve followed on 14 September. The rules had changed in three places by then, so that batch was replayed under the literal frozen code; the current rules decide identically on every run in both batches.

Failure 1, and how the retry fixes it

Q. Does every Rivenhall listing stock the 2 x 9.5L pack? A. Yes Wrong. Five of the fifteen do not stock it, and the agent had opened four. No hedge, no qualification. It answered this way three separate times.
A, second attempt. No. Out of 15 total listings, only 10 stock this pack size. The 5 that do not are: Chandler Wholesale, Pembroke Trading, ... Correct, with its evidence, three times out of three. The fix does not tell the agent the answer. It tells it that a claim about "every listing" cannot rest on four of them.

The one that escaped, and why it matters

Caught: "...at $0.930/L. This was determined by analyzing all four sellers" Escaped: "...at $0.930/L for the 2 x 9.5L pack (total price: $17.68 for 19 litres)" Same wrong answer, same four listings opened. The only difference is that the second one did not volunteer how much it had checked. The rule reads a claim the agent makes about itself, so an agent that says less about itself is checked less. That is a real ceiling on the method, not a bug in it, and it is written down as one.

The obvious fix, rejected on a miscount

Requiring the agent to open enough listings would catch the escapee. It was first tested and rejected on counts that missed listings opened by clicking a card. Counted correctly, over every unmodified run of the comparison questions on the current draw:

Listings opened, by whether the answer was right
OutcomeListings opened, each runConsequence of a rule at 11
Correct answers4, 4, 14, 15, 15, 15, 15, 15, 15Two would be blocked, each opened 4 of 15
Wrong answers4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4, 4All nine would be caught

The earlier counts were 1, 4, 4, 8, 12, 13, 14 and 14 for correct answers, which made the rule look costly. The rejection no longer holds as first stated. Whether to add a coverage rule, and at what floor for each kind of question, is open: refuting a claim about every listing needs one counterexample, not eleven openings.

The same numbers still give something more useful than the rule would have. The cheapest-pack question was answered correctly twice after opening 4 of 15 listings, below the 11 any verified answer needs: the winner happened to sit among the four opened. Those answers were not reasoned to, they were landed on. The per-litre question opens exactly 4 listings in all nine runs and is wrong in all nine. A score out of ten cannot tell those apart, because there is nothing in the run to tell apart, which is the argument for reporting whether an answer was earned next to whether it was right.

7. Main challenges

What went wrong, and what it cost
ChallengeWhat happenedResolution
Cheap models could not do the taskThe proposal assumed a cheap model tier. One could not follow the required output format, others were blocked by their providers regardless of account creditEverything runs on a model that works and is not free. This is the direct cause of the spend
Silent environment failureThe shop reported itself healthy while its search engine was hung, so the catalogue looked empty for no visible reasonDiagnosed and documented. Two wrong theories were tried first, and are recorded as wrong
Two bugs inside the agentA valid empty result was reported as a parse error, and a tool failure did not reach the caller, which then reported successBoth patched locally. Candidates for contributing back, since the agent is open source
I misdiagnosed a failure as fabricationReported that the agent invented prices. It had not. $15.01 is a real surcharge on a $9.89 base, and 9.89 + 15.01 = 24.90, which is the true price. It misread the meaning of a number it had read correctlyCorrected in every document. The distinction matters: a misreading is fixable by a rule, an invention is not
My marking rules were wrong twiceOne penalised an answer for mentioning the alternatives, which is what a good comparison does. Another penalised an answer for showing its own arithmeticBoth fixed and all stored runs re-marked at no cost. One already-written claim had to be withdrawn
My own check blocked a correct answer"$17.68, Redhill Stores" is right, but $17.68 never appears on the page, being a $6.82 base plus a $10.86 surcharge. The check only trusted answers that spelled out the sumFixed, which cost some detection power, because two of its catches turned out to be accidental. Losing an accidental catch is the correct trade
Runs scored against a moved answer keyThe market is regenerated between rounds, so older runs were being marked against prices that no longer appliedDetected automatically by re-marking and checking for disagreement. Four runs excluded. Without this the method looked worse than it is

Five of these seven are mistakes I made rather than problems I encountered. They are listed because each one changed a number that had already been written down, and the record of catching them is part of the work.
