# The claim run, protocol

Written on 20 October 2026 at noon, before the claim market exists, and finalised the same
afternoon from the verifier's logic audit, still before the market existed. Two things are
filled in at the verifier's stamp: the versions in the frozen list, and the exact command.
Nothing else in this file changes after the stamp.

## The question it answers

Research question 3 on a market the block was not designed on: does the block reach correct
answers while opening fewer listings than exhaustive exploration? And research question 2
again on that market: does the shipped agent fail there as it did on Thornwood?

## What is frozen (final)

Everything at the commit the verifier stamps for the claim run. Its versions are written here
at the stamp. Expected: box v10 (strategy text v8, form with a strict "under"), scorer v3,
openJiuwen at commit 18d45d6 with the recorded local patch (full-index digest e455d3eb…),
@playwright/mcp 0.0.83 with `--allowed-origins http://localhost:7770
--block-service-workers --isolated`, Claude Haiku 4.5 through OpenRouter at the agent's own
sampling (temperature 0.2, top_p 0.1), 100 steps, 600 seconds.

Amended on 20 October, before the claim market was generated, after the verifier's logic
audit: one false sentence in the strategy text corrected (it said nothing in a listing can
cost less than its card, which a sold-out size can), "under $27" read strictly as the keys
already read it, a fresh browser profile for every run (every run since 8 September had
shared one, cookies and session included), and a fourth arm.

If a fault is found in any of these after the claim market is generated, it is recorded,
fixed, and the claim is re-run on another fresh market generated by the same rule. The fix
is never tested on this one.

## The market (final)

- **Generator.** `env/generate_apparel.py` with its 13 acceptance rules unchanged, four sizes
  (S, M, L, XL), 15 listings, the same price model as Thornwood.
- **Page rule.** `env/pick_apparel_market.py`'s rule, unchanged in what it states: the answer
  renders outside the first four cards, and a listing inside the first four advertises less
  than the answer costs. Its implementation is corrected (20 October, before any seed was
  generated) to read the price the card advertises, the lowest buyable size, where the code
  had taken the lowest of all sizes including sold-out ones.
- **Seed rule.** Seeds 204, 205, 206 and on, in that order (Thornwood was 203). The claim
  market is the first seed that passes the 13 rules and the page rule. No candidate is
  rejected by hand. Every seed tried is recorded with its outcome.
- **Brand rule.** The first of Ashcombe, Hartwell, Wyndham that the shop's search returns no
  product for before seeding, and that is no word of any seller name the generator can draw
  (amended 20 October, before any seed was generated: Kelsall was removed because it is a
  seller first name in the generator's pool, and the scorer matches a seller by its first
  word, so a Kelsall brand would score every answer as naming a Kelsall seller).
- **Thornwood stays.** The claim market uses its own SKU prefix and URL keys, so Thornwood's
  15 listings, its fixture and its task files are untouched, and the development batches
  stay reproducible.
- **Checked in the live shop before any run.** The results page for the brand lists exactly
  the 15 listings with no next page. Every card's advertised price equals the fixture's
  lowest buyable price for that listing (the bound the block relies on). Every size's label,
  price and salability equals the fixture's. The gated theme shows only the advertised price
  until a size is chosen. Thornwood's results page is unchanged.

## Questions (final)

The six questions the Thornwood cells asked (the results-page control and the five gated
questions), worded identically apart from the brand. Keys and the buyable reading of the two
"offer" questions are computed from the claim fixture by code, with the task file. The newer
question shapes follow the claim run as their own batch. They are not part of it, because
they have one development run each and the rule-based parser is weakest on them.

## Arms (final)

Every arm runs Claude Haiku 4.5 at the agent's own sampling, 100 steps, 600 seconds, the
same browser flags, a fresh profile, a per-run selector cache, and the block's answer check
off. Only the arm varies.

1. **Shipped.** openJiuwen as shipped, as cell 1. *Why:* research question 2 on a held-out
   market, and the floor.
2. **Open everything.** As shipped, with cell 5's instruction to open every listing, word for
   word, on the five gated questions as cell 5. *Why:* the exhaustive baseline research
   question 3 names.
3. **The block.** Box v10 on the six questions. Its question about "offer" is answered with the
   reading the key was written under (menu), and the two "offer" questions run again under
   the other reading (buyable). *Why:* the contribution, and the ask-back shown to resolve
   either reading.
4. **The block without its stop rule** (`--box-exhaust`). The same text, except that every
   gated question says to open every listing. It runs on target-price, cheapest-target and
   all-stocked, where the stop rule acts. On the two count questions the block already opens
   everything and the text is byte-identical, so the block's runs stand for both. *Why:* on
   Thornwood, 41 per cent of the calls the block saved against open everything were
   results-page work it does for the agent by handing over the listings. This arm separates
   that from the stop rule, which is the policy research question 3 is about.

The answer check takes no browser action, so replaying it from the logs afterwards gives the
same verdict as running it live, with the version frozen at the claim commit.

## Order (final)

Three rounds. Each round runs every arm once (`--repeats 1 --repeat-start r`), and the order
of the arms rotates. Round 1 runs shipped, open everything, the block (menu, then buyable),
then the block without its stop rule. Round 2 starts at open everything, and round 3 starts
at the block. All of it runs as one guarded command that names `suite.py` for every
invocation. *Why:* any drift in the model endpoint, and the first run's cache write after a
pause, then fall on every arm alike, and the spend guard sees every paid call.

## Measures (final)

- **Primary 1, correct.** Scored under the key fixed before the run, on the four gated
  questions whose answer does not depend on how "offer" is read: target-price,
  cheapest-target, all-stocked and count-buyable. *Why:* on count-stocked the key's reading
  decides who is right (cell 5 scored 0 of 3 under menu and 3 of 3 under buyable), and only
  the block is told the reading.
- **Primary 2, listings opened.** Counted by membership of the market's 15 URLs, in every arm.
- **Primary 3, cost per run.** The provider's charge.
- **Secondary:**
  - count-stocked as a table of arm by key, with the reading each answer declares, coded by
    hand without seeing the arm, and the block's ask counted as one user turn
  - opened what the answer needs, as which listings, from the floors
  - browser actions on the results page and on listings
  - asks on the clear and on the ambiguous questions
  - the answer check replayed: wrong answers refused, correct answers refused
  - step-budget and clock hits
  - runs that read the hidden prices in the page source (the leak counter)
  - listings opened outside the market
  - the stop rule's own effect, the block against the block without its stop rule.

## Analysis (final)

Per question first, then pooled. The unit is a run, and distinct runs are reported.

- **Primary tests,** with Holm's correction over the three:
  - **H1:** on the four reading-invariant gated questions, the block is correct more often
    than shipped. Two-sided stratified exact test by question
    (`compare_cells.stratified_exact`).
  - **H2:** the block opens fewer of the market's listings than open everything. Two-sided
    stratified exact rank test within question (van Elteren with equal weights and
    mid-ranks).
  - **H3:** the block costs less per run than open everything. Same test as H2.
- **Correctness against open everything** is shown as counts with Newcombe 95 per cent
  intervals. Fifteen runs per arm cannot show two arms are equal, so it is never reported
  as equivalence.
- **Secondary and exploratory:** the stop rule's effect (the block against the block without
  it, on correct, listings opened and cost). Everything else is descriptive.
- **Sensitivity:** without both "offer" questions, count-stocked under each reading,
  distinct runs only, and with the voids included.

## Exclusions (final)

Each is decided from the log and the run record before the answer is read.

- **Void:** a silence over 60 seconds (the laptop slept), or a provider or runner error with
  no final completion. A void is re-run once after round 3, and a second void is reported as
  missing.
- **Not correct:** the step or clock limit, no answer, or an abstention. These are reported
  separately.
- Nothing else is excluded.

## Limits stated in advance (final)

- The question templates are in sample: the rules were written on them. The market is not.
- One model and one scaffold. The second agent cannot run behind the block until its browser
  is confined to the shop.
- Markets are generated so that a card-only answer is wrong, and the market is selected so
  that the first screen misleads. The claim is conditional on gating being present. How
  often it is present in real shops is research question 1, answered separately. One market
  is one draw.
- The stop rule assumes a card advertises no more than the cheapest size that can be bought.
  That holds by construction here and is checked live. In a real shop it can break, for
  example through a stale price index, a default-variant card, or member prices.
- "Not shown" for a sold-out size's price means not in anything the agent observed. The
  price is in the page source, base64-encoded, and the leak counter reports any run that
  read it.
- The rule-based parser gives the template's kind to 17 of 30 paraphrases written blind by
  the verifier. The claim is about the six templates, not about free wording.
- The answer check cannot see a price misread (an advertised price reported as a size's
  price) or a miscount once every listing is opened. Its replay is reported with these blind
  spots.
- The browser flags (allowed origins, blocked service workers, a fresh profile) differ from
  the Thornwood cells' conditions. All the claim's arms run under the same flags.
