In one paragraph A shopping agent answers from the page it can see. When the price, size or stock a question needs only appears after a click, it often answers anyway, confidently and wrongly. Two weeks of tests say where the failure lives and what it costs to avoid. The block built this week sits before the agent: it reads the question and the first page, hands the agent a plan, asks back when the question is ambiguous, and checks the answer from the page. Its pilot got both questions right, opening listings instead of 15 on the cheapest question for against about $0.59. Version 2, built today, covers every question shape the shopping benchmarks contain, with a model doing the parsing and rules doing everything after.

2. Building the block

What it does, in three moves

PlanReads the question and the first results page, and hands the agent the listing count, the listings by advertised price with their URLs, and a strategy with a stop rule: open these, in this order, stop when no unopened one can change the answer.
Ask, or push back, or pass throughWhen the question has two readings, asks which. When it names something the shop does not show, says so and runs nothing. When it is not about shopping at all, hands it to the agent unchanged and counts it.
CheckAfter the answer, decides from the page alone whether the answer is supported by what was opened, and refuses it if not. No answer key is ever read.
Version 1 and version 2Version 1 ran the pilot above with four fixed strategies. Version 2, built today after the research below, turns any question into a logical form, lets a model do that parsing, verifies it by five properties, and composes the plan from seven primitives. On the six clothing questions it produces byte for byte what version 1 produced, so the pilot stands.

The block fills the space between them.

Architecture

Click a step. Green is the block's own code, yellow where it stops to ask, blue the agent, amber the shop.

Should the block be an agent?A model parses the question, because that is judgement, and on 37 questions it agrees with the rules on the plan-deciding fields of times. Rules do everything after, because their stop rules are theorems, their checks can refuse a confident answer, and the same bytes come out every time. A fully agentic block would be Test 4 with extra steps, at times the cost, and Test 4 is what the block has to beat.

Why this matters, and what it is not

Agent agnostic

Not a browser agent, and not replacing one

  • The block sits in front of whichever agent is used and hands it text.
  • Test 3 is the evidence that the two errors it targets besides coverage, the ambiguous verb and the price read too early, appear in a second agent built from scratch.
  • The block in front of that agent is next week's check.
Answering, not buying

The step before the purchase button

  • Every purchase an agent makes is a retrieval question first: which offer, at what price, in which size, in stock.
  • A wrong answer there becomes a wrong purchase one click later. The live assistant seen in August named one seller 27% cheaper for a carton it was dearer for, with no sign of doubt.
  • Buying, discounts, coupons and delivery are out of scope and stated as limits. Reviews are in scope and come next.
